\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Deep Equilibrium Models and Monotone Constraints.}
Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQs) \cite{bai2019deep} reformulate deep networks by solving for implicit fixed points $z^* = f_\theta(z^*, x)$ rather than evaluating explicit feedforward layers. To guarantee global fixed-point existence and numerical convergence during forward and backward passes, subsequent architectures enforce strict structural constraints. Monotone operator equilibrium networks \cite{winston2020monotone} enforce strong monotonicity, and Lipschitz-certified equilibrium models \cite{revay2020lipschitz} parameterize networks with prescribed Lipschitz bounds; in the contractive regime ($\text{Lip}<1$) these guarantees are obtained. While these constraints yield well-posed forward solvers and stable implicit differentiation, in that regime, Banach's Fixed-Point Theorem mathematically restricts the system to a single global attractor ($N=1$). Consequently, monotone DEQs are structurally incapable of representing multistable physical systems or mapping energy barriers between distinct metastable states.

\paragraph{Physics-Informed Equilibrium and Conservative Networks.}
To incorporate physical conservation laws into neural representations, Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs) \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian} and Lagrangian Neural Networks \cite{cranmer2020lagrangian} learn scalar energy functions $H(q, p)$ whose gradients govern conservative time evolution. However, classical HNNs operate primarily as explicit ODE integrators rather than implicit equilibrium models, and require full phase-space position and momentum pairs during training. EquiPhase DEQ extends conservative potential learning into the implicit equilibrium setting by coupling periodic neural potentials with damped velocity Verlet steps, replacing exact conservation with a controlled dissipative structure ($J^\top \Omega J = (1-\eta)\Omega$) so that trajectories converge to attractors while inheriting the geometric stability of symplectic integration.

\paragraph{Score-Based Modeling and Molecular Energy Landscapes.}
Denoising Score Matching (DSM) \cite{song2019generative, song2021scorebased} estimates the score field $\nabla_q \log p(q)$ of a target data distribution without computing intractable partition functions. In computational biophysics, score-based models and Markov State Models such as VAMPnets \cite{mardt2018vampnets, wu2020variational} have been applied to identify metastable states and their interconversion kinetics from high-dimensional molecular dynamics trajectories. While unconstrained score networks can accurately fit local density gradients, their estimated vector fields generally exhibit non-zero curl ($\nabla \times S \neq 0$), rendering them non-conservative and preventing the extraction of a single consistent scalar potential $V(q)$. EquiPhase DEQ resolves this by constructing $S(q) = -\nabla_q V_\theta(q)$ structurally, guaranteeing zero curl and a valid free energy readout.
